The קאַליך רומשינסקי finding. This node looks exactly like a two-word spelling variant, and a naive surname-based dedup would swallow it. It is not one. Miriam Shomer Zunser's entry records that in 1931 the theatre passed אונטער דער דירעקציע פֿון קאַליך רומשינסקי — the Jacob Kalich + Rumshinsky management duo, a compound of two surnames. The same list carries רומשינסקי-סקולניק, the same construction with Menashe Skulnik. The network page keeps its edge, attributes it to the ego, and labels it managed (with Jacob Kalich) rather than pretending it is a spelling of his name; Kalich himself (person:922) is already a colleague_of neighbour, described in his own edge as רומשינסקיס לאַנג-יאָריקער מיטאַרבעטער און שותף אין טעאַטער.

Generalizable: in this corpus a bare surname pair is as likely to be a management partnership as a name variant. Duo-surface strings deserve their own detector before any surname dedup runs.

3 · Life & career course

A Gemini pass over the 71,867-character entry yielded 20 stations carrying 15 events, across 12 distinct places. All 20 place strings resolved against the gazetteer — nothing was left unresolved. Four stations have no stated date and were interpolated from narrative order; they are drawn faded on both the timeline and the map.

The route: Vilna → cantorial tours through Russia → Grodno → Dvinsk → Borisov → Yelisavetgrad → Łódź (הזמיר) → Warsaw Conservatory → London (1903–4) → New York (July 1904) → Boston (1905–6) → back to New York for the Windsor, Thalia, National and Second Avenue theatres → a 1921 European visit → Maurice Schwartz's Yiddish Art Theatre (1946–9) → death, New York, 1956.

The single KG-only row is explainable, not a contradiction. E-13417 dates his takeover of the Second Avenue directorship to 1931; the extraction closed his Second Avenue station at 1919–1930 and marked that end date approximate. The two layers describe the same engagement and disagree only about where its open end falls — which is precisely what an approximate date means. No anchor disagrees about place.

Five extraction-only stations — the Russian cantorial tour (1890–4), Dvinsk (1896), Borisov (1897), London (1903–4) and the 1921 European visit — are dated stations the KG has no fact for. They are not errors; they mark where the narrative is richer than the extracted graph. The London and Dvinsk years in particular are entirely absent from the KG.

Caveat on org-derived places. An anchor's place comes from its organization's located_in edge, which records an org's home base. For a touring troupe that is misleading: E-13411 resolves Kaminski's troupe to Warsaw, while the entry has him joining its choir on tour in Grodno. The verdict still reads match because he was demonstrably with that troupe — but for itinerant organizations, "located_in" is not a station.

A gazetteer bug found on the way — since fixed upstream. באָריסאָוו was reachable in toponyms_gazetteer.csv only through the wrong row: it sat as a variant of Babruysk (Q207294), while the correct Barysaw/Borisov row (Q19313) carried no coordinates and so was skipped by every resolver. The first pass therefore sent his 1897 Borisov season to Babruysk. Traced to a single attestation in places_unified_corrected.csv — Avrom Litman's birthplace, clustered correctly as "Barysaw" but stamped with Babruysk's QID and self-flagged qid_source: dodgy. Corrected at source: the attestation is re-linked to Q19313, three sibling rows that still named Babruysk as their settlement were repaired, Barysaw's coordinates were added to settlement_coords.tsv, and all four are pinned in manual_qid_locks.csv so a clean regeneration cannot undo them. Both spellings now resolve to Barysaw with coordinates from the gazetteer alone, and the dossier no longer needs a local override.

Related, still open. That row's qid_source: dodgy flag is not rare: 1,145 of the 5,690 rows in places_unified_corrected.csv carry it, and in 470 of them the clustered place name disagrees outright with the Wikidata label attached to it — Atlantic City linked to "Deutsche Post AG", Harbin to "Isuzu", Tiraspol to "Emily Blunt". toponyms_misresolved.csv catches only the 15 whose target type is obviously not a place. The remainder is a separate cleanup worth its own pass.

Deliberately not used: the genre column of eval/eval_reference_works.tsv. It is held-out evaluation data; joining against it here would contaminate the eval. Genres were instead sanity-checked with a nikud-tolerant regex over the Yiddish genre vocabulary. It disagreed with the model on three works, and in all three cases the regex was more specific: a waltz and a fantasia the model filed as "other", and one אָפּערעסע — an OCR slip for אָפּערעטע — that it read as "opera".

6 · Method & gaps

How this was built

  • Network — build_person_network.py over kg/nodes.tsv + kg/edges.tsv; vis-network from CDN with an inline payload (53 KB, not a 3 MB pyvis dump). Variant merges are display-level only.
  • Itinerary — one Gemini call (gemini-3-flash-preview, temperature 0) through the shared itinerary/extract_itineraries.py, which gained --ids-file, --out and --prompt-extra so the Istanbul pilot's own behaviour is unchanged. The extra prompt told it the subject is a composer, so study and employment stations count, not only tours.
  • Resolution — build_person_itinerary_data.py, a local copy of the pilot resolver with a per-dossier place supplement, plus the new kg_anchors agreement pass.
  • Works — extract_works.py: one Gemini call, then the attestation guard, the KG join and the genre regex, all deterministic.
  • Pages — build_person_pages.py, adapted from build_istanbul_pages.py; the timeline is re-laned by place rather than by person, since a person dossier has one career.
  • Entry panel — make_entry_json.py, a reusable generator (the Istanbul pilot's equivalent file was hand-made). His own entry ships in full; the 120 mentioning entries ship as ±900-character windows, which keeps the payload at 836 KB instead of 8.9 MB.

Known gaps

  • 110 of his 117 named works are not in the plays layer. The largest single coverage gap this dossier surfaces.
  • Alternate titles are not modelled. קלוגע פרויען / חכמת נשים and שמענדריקס חתונה / המן דער צווייטער are the same works under two names; only the evidence sentence connects them. play:NEW-0032 needs re-checking — its title appears nowhere in the entry that sourced it.
  • Burial place is unrepresented. The extractor has slots for settlements, not for an institution's cemetery.
  • Org located_in is a home base, not a station — misleading for touring troupes (the Kaminski case above).
  • באָריסאָוו — fixed upstream (see §3). What remains is the wider problem it exposed: 470 qid_source: dodgy rows whose place name and Wikidata label disagree.
  • Edge ids shift on every rebuild. Anything that cites them — including these dossiers — must be re-verified afterwards. Stable fact identifiers would be worth having.
  • Five dated stations have no KG fact behind them (Russia 1890–4, Dvinsk 1896, Borisov 1897, London 1903–4, Europe 1921): candidates for promotion into the graph.
  • Family relations are shown but not analysed; his second wife and daughter remain unmatched UP- nodes, as do 24 of his 72 neighbours.

Everything here is reproducible: Zylbercweig/plays/person_dossier/ holds the config, the four builders, and the committed Gemini outputs (rumshinsky_stations.jsonl, rumshinsky_works.json), so the pages rebuild without re-calling the model. Point the same scripts at another *.json config to build the next person dossier; set has_works: false and §4 simply does not run.
