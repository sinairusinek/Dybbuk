Data problems found — and fixed

All four problems this dossier first reported were fixed on 2026-11-15 (details in §9–§10).

  1. Lateiner's comedy was credited to his wife. The possessive „לאַטיינערס" had auto-linked to Izela Lateiner (person:927), so she "authored" ten of his plays, the Dybbuk among them. Five more spellings (יוסף לאַטיינער, י. לאטיינער …) had minted stray "unmatched" authors. Fixed: all six spellings are ALIGNed to Joseph Lateiner (person:683) in kg_link_review.tsv, and 26 author edges moved.
  2. The Odessa date was an OCR error, not a KG error. The transcription of Mogulesko's entry read אין אָדעס אין 1889; the printed page (vol. 2, cols. 1183–1184) reads 1880, as the KG event EV-0194 already had it. Fixed in the vol. 2 TEI and entry_texts.tsv. Blank's entry (vol. 1, cols. 171–172) really does print 1889 for the Lateiner–Mogulesko troupe in Odessa. That is the Leksikon's own claim and it stands as such.
  3. The Danzig dossier cited deleted events. Fixed: the page now explains that both Danzig events (the Dybbuk and „גרינע פֿעלדער") left the L/H KG with the 11-01 attribution guard, and links here.
  4. Premiere place and four impossible dates. Nachbush's entry made the Vilna Troupe premiere happen "in Vilna", and four stagings were dated 1914–16, before the play existed. In each case the extractor took the actor's engagement date or the troupe's name. Fixed by hand corrections in build_dybbuk_pages.py, listed in §10.

"Called the Dybbuk" covers more than one play. Lateiner's comedy also goes by its subtitle די באָבע חוה. Ansky's play goes by its first title, צווישן צוויי וועלטן (Between Two Worlds), and in Hebrew הדיבוק. Shomer's play was a pastiche of three Goldfadn pieces released under the name דער דיבוק. The parodies have their own titles: מיטן כּח פֿון דבוק, דבוקיאַדע, די דריי דבוקים, and Modicut's puppet דבוק. Rocca's opera Il Dibuk (1934) reached the Warsaw Opera. The 1937 film is Waszyński's, from Alter Kacyzne's screenplay. Ansky's other play, טאָג און נאַכט, is listed in §10 but is not a Dybbuk.

3 · S. An-ski, „דער דיבוק“ / „צווישן צוויי וועלטן“

The tables below follow the play from Ansky's writing (1911–14, Tarnów in his own entry) and Bialik's Hebrew translation (Vilna, 1918), through the Habima studio rehearsals in Moscow (Stanislavski, then Vakhtangov, with designs by Altman and music by Engel), to the Vilna Troupe premiere in Warsaw on 9 December 1920, directed by David Herman with music by M. Shneur. They then trace its spread: Maurice Schwartz's Yiddish Art Theatre in New York (1921, music by Cherniavsky), the Vilna Troupe's own tours to New York, London, Western Europe and Romania, Polish, German and English versions, provincial troupes from Łódź to Kishinev and Buenos Aires, and the Łódź ghetto. Most of it falls in the 1920s.

4 · Joseph Lateiner, „דער דיבוק, אָדער די באָבע חוה“

Lateiner's third play, written in Iași (his own entry dates it 1877). The entries tell of stagings in Botoșani (Juvelier as Falik, with Itsik-Mendl Berman's troupe) and Odessa (Mogulesko as Bobe Khave), with Gimpel's troupe in Lemberg (Lager as Falek, 1888), and of Shoengold "as the lover", recalled by B. Weinstein. This is the only Dybbuk in the plays KG (see §9). Its characters are encoded in the YiDraCor edition, YiDraCor/tei/Dybbuk4Dracor.xml.

6 · Parodies and travesties

The Dybbuk's success bred parodies almost at once. In Warsaw, Kipnis and Yeushson's מיטן כּח פֿון דבוק premiered on 3 April 1921, directed by Samberg. Shoshana's די דריי דבוקים was staged with Morris Lampe and Lazar Kahan.
