Extraction schema, v2

The schema of the Lexicon extraction: nine kinds of entity and the relations between them, as it stands. All seven volumes are extracted — 7,003 parts over 3,071 entries — and each entity below carries what it actually holds. Solid lines are in the data; dotted lines are not built yet. Click any entity or relation for its fields, its counts and the vocabularies it uses.

What is still open is resolution, not extraction: surname ER (step 9), production grouping (9b), person→org enrichment (8g), graph assembly (10) and publication (11).

Entity cards — one card per entity kind, with its fields and the relations going out and coming in. The plain-text form of an entity–relationship diagram.

Class diagram (UML-style, also called an ERD) — each box is an entity kind: its name, then its fields. Lines are associations, labelled with the relation; arrows point from the subject to the object. The stereotype line says «in the data» or «not built yet».

Chen notation (the original entity–relationship notation, 1976) — rectangles are entities, diamonds are relationships, ovals are attributes (fields). It puts the relations on a par with the entities, which suits a schema where relations carry their own fields.

Adjacency matrix — rows are the subject of a relation, columns its object; each cell lists the relation families between the two. The densest form, with no crossing lines. Hover a relation for its types.

Concept map — concepts in boxes, joined by linking phrases that read as sentences (“a Lexicon entry is about a person”). Less formal, for explaining the schema to people who don’t read diagrams.

Inventory & work list: what earlier extraction runs already produced (do not re-extract), and the seven v2 jobs. Jobs A–C work on sentences or fields already extracted and need no re-read; D–G share one full-text pass over the entries not yet covered, whose output omits everything in the inventory.

How to read it

Everything is extracted as written: names as they appear, with a short verbatim quote as evidence. Person mentions and person ↔ person relations already exist for all seven volumes (people/mentions_all.tsv) and are not re-extracted. Within one entry, job A resolves פּ., ער and short surnames to the fullest form in that entry. Which person a name refers to across the Lexicon is decided afterwards, in entity resolution, which uses these relations as evidence.

Person → organization relations (18.5k edges, aligned through core_db) are not re-extracted. “X played in Y’s troupe” becomes two such edges — Y leads the troupe, X performed in it — not a person-to-person link.

Where the text rarely decides a distinction (theatre building vs company, hall vs theatre), the schema has no field for it; what the text does state is recorded as dated facts instead.

Anything that does not fit the schema goes to schema_issues, with a question.
