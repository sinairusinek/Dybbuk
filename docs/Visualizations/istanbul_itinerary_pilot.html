Itinerary Extraction Pilot

What one Gemini pass over the 79 Istanbul-linked Leksikon entries taught us about extracting itineraries and events corpus-wide — and how the two relate.

1 · The headline: coverage multiplier vs. the current KG

For Istanbul, the KG previously held 5 person↔place facts and 1 event. The pilot extracted 88 Istanbul stations (≈18×) and 45 events located there (≈45×) — from the same source text, at flash-model cost, in ~15 minutes. The itinerary layer is not a marginal improvement; it is most of the person–place signal in the corpus.

2 · Itineraries vs. events: the model that worked

We treated stations (entity × place × interval, in narrative order) as the spine and nested events (point occurrences: performance, founding, marriage…) inside the station where they happen. In practice this dissolved the overlap completely: nothing was extracted twice, every event automatically carries a location and a host itinerary, and a performance mention still yields a station even when no explicit travel verb appears. The timeline draws events over station bands; the map draws stations — same data, no reconciliation step.

3 · Dating: narrative order carries the load

Roughly 40% of stations have an explicit or approximate date in the text. about 58% received an inferred year-range interpolated from the nearest dated neighbors in narrative order (Leksikon entries are chronological); about 3% are undatable. Spot checks (Zbarzher, Grinberg, Amatin) show the interpolated ranges are coherent. Recommendation: keep inferred ranges as first-class but flagged data (t_inferred) — they are what makes a timeline possible at all.

The gazetteer's attested-variant field did the heavy lifting — points-stripped exact match resolved 70% with zero fuzzy logic. Corpus-wide, new variants surfacing here should feed the existing kimatch review flow, not a new mechanism.

5 · Mention ≠ visit (and extraction recall)

Of the 72 unique careers in the cohort (79 entries minus 7 cross-volume duplicate pairs, merged), 9 mention Istanbul without ever being there: scholarship about Constantinople (Shimen Ernst), the operetta title די רויז פֿון סטאַמבול in repertoire lists (Kadish, Zeiderman, Dina Kenig, Hershele), a play about traffic to Constantinople (Lilienblum), or a relative's birthplace (Gitl Shteyn's father — captured as his own third-person station). A text-mention cohort therefore overstates presence by ~12%; the station extraction is what separates the two, and the viz pages hide mention-only careers behind a toggle. Two extraction lessons from auditing this class: one long entry (Weinblatt) initially skipped a whole journey (Odessa→Romania→Constantinople→back) — long entries need a recall verification pass, as in the plays-KG dual-pass protocol; and one response nested its JSON one level deep — the parser must accept all three shapes seen (object, bare list, wrapped list).

6 · Third-person testimony is real data

22 of 101 extracted itineraries belong to people other than the entry subject — Kornfeld's entry alone narrates Hurwitz's, Jawiler's, Gelis's and Krauze's Istanbul visits. The schema's subject field captured this cleanly. Corpus-wide this is a ~20% data bonus, but it adds a required step: resolving free-text subject names ("פּראָפעסאָר הורוויץ") against people_db — the same entity-linking machinery the mentions layer already uses.

7 · Confirmed traps (all handled, all will recur)

  • Work titles containing toponyms (די רויז פֿון סטאַמבול) and toponymic surnames (סטאַמבולקאַ) — excluded by prompt rule; keep it.
  • Hebrew points must be stripped before any matching; OCR confusions (ג↔נ: Galata as נאָלאַטאָ) resolve via the variant table, not normalization.
  • The Leksikon repeats people across volumes (7 pairs in this cohort: Leresko, Katia Adler, Rokhl & Ch.-D. Ariel, Sonia Amatin, Mendelevitsh, Hershele) — merged person-level in the viz (richer extraction kept, labeled "vols 1+5"); corpus run needs the same merge step.
  • Venue-level "places" (schools, theaters, wine cellars) belong to a venue_only place_kind, not the settlement resolver.
  • One of 79 responses came back as a bare JSON list rather than an object — parser must accept both shapes.

8 · Proposed corpus-wide plan

  1. Extract all ~3,000 person entries with the pilot schema (est. ~15–20k stations, ~8–10k events; flash-tier cost is negligible; a few hours parallelized).
  2. Resolve places against the toponym spine; route new variants into the kimatch review queue (existing donation flow).
  3. Link orgs: station org strings → org core_db clusters via the existing blocking/translit machinery. Org itineraries then come free, by aggregating member stations — no separate org extraction needed at first.
  4. Link third-person subjects → people_db (reuse mentions-layer matching).
  5. Integrate into the KG: stations become person→place edges (stayed_in / toured_in / passed_through / settled_in / fled_to) carrying dates, certainty, inferred-flag and evidence; events become event nodes linked to their station edge. The existing born_in/died_in bio edges stay as-is — they are just the first two verb classes of the same model.
  6. Evaluate with per-city dossiers (this Istanbul set becomes the gold sample: hand-verify ~10 entries for precision/recall before the full run).
Open questions for the team: (a) should region-level tour stations enter the KG as edges to region entities, or only annotate the neighboring settlement edges? (b) do we want inferred dates in the KG proper, or only in derived views? (c) who reviews the ~12% unresolved-place residue — auto-queue to the RA place flow, or leave unresolved with the evidence quote?
