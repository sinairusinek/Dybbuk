- **Part A** — the tags you already use, written out in plain words, with the actual

- **Part B** — a handful of **new tags** the material seems to be asking for.

A troupe can carry **several tags at once** — most of yours do. A company can be a
*Star* troupe *and* an *Operetta* troupe *and* a *Family* troupe. The tags answer
different questions; they don't compete.

## Part A — the tags we already use, and how to spot them

### Impresario Company  ·  *the everyday base tag*
**Plain meaning:** a touring company named after the one person who runs it and hires
the actors. This is the ordinary Yiddish travelling troupe, and it fits almost every
eponymous "So-and-so's Troupe."

**Signs in the entry:**
- The name is one surname + *troupe*: *לערמאַנס טרופּע*, *עבעלס טרופּע*, *טיטעלמאַנס טרופּע*.
- Actors are **hired into it**: *אָנגאַזשירט*, *טרעט אַריין אין… טרופּע*.
- It is run **under one person's direction**: *אונטער… אָנפֿירונג / לייטונג*, *דער דירעקטאָר פֿון דער טרופּע*.

**Your example:** *ראַפּעלס טרופּע*, *ליפּאָווסקיס טרופּע* — Impresario Company.
*(This is why 26 of your 28 carry it: it's the default, and the other tags add colour on top.)*

### Star Company
**Plain meaning:** the company exists **around one famous actor** — the draw is the
performer, not the management.

**Signs in the entry:**
- The named person is a well-known **actor** (not just a manager), and others "play
  **with** them": *געשפּילט מיט אַדלערן איבער דער פּראָווינץ*, *אַ סעזאָן מיט אָדלער*.
- People join to appear **under the star**: *אונטער דער לייטונג פֿון דזשייקאָב אַדלער*.

**Your example:** *Jacob P. Adler*, *Sarah Adler* — Star Company.

### Family Company
**Plain meaning:** a theatre **family** performing as one unit.

**Signs in the entry:**
- More than one relative of the same name: *די ברידער קאָריק* (the Korik brothers);
  both *אסתר-רחל קאַמינסקאַ* **and** *אידא קאַמינסקאַ* (mother and daughter).
- A known theatrical dynasty (Kaminski, Adler, Turkow, Fishzon…).

**Your example:** *Kaminska Troupe*, *קאָריקס טרופּע* — Family Company.

### Operetta / Opera Company
**Plain meaning:** the company is known for **operetta / musical theatre**, not spoken drama.

**Signs in the entry:**
- The word *אָפּערעטע*, a named operetta (*הורוויץס אָפּערעטע עתליהו*), or a **prima donna**: *פּרימאַדאָנע*.
- Directors from the operetta/*shund* world — Hurwitz, Shomer (Shaykevitsh), Fishzon.

**Your example:** *Hurwitz Troupe*, *Shomer Troupe* — Operetta / Opera Company.

### Ad Hoc Company
**Plain meaning:** a **temporary combination** of two or more principals who joined
forces for a while, rather than a single standing company.

**Signs in the entry:**
- The name is **two people joined**: *ראָזענבערג-ספּיוואָקאָווסקיס טרופּע*, *X מיט Y*.
- The text describes them **starting to play together**: *ראָזענבערג מיט ספּיוואָקאָווסקין הויבן אָן שפּילן*.

**Your example:** *Rosenberg and Spivakovski Troupe*, *Fishzon-Spivakovski Troupe* — Ad Hoc.

### Ensemble Company
**Plain meaning:** a **standing, named company** that worked as a settled group over
years — even if two names are in its title, it is stable, not a one-off pairing.
*(This is the fine line against Ad Hoc: Ad Hoc = they teamed up for a season;
Ensemble = they became a lasting company.)*

**Signs in the entry:**
- A named troupe that recurs over a long span: *טרופּע קאַדיש-כאַש* across 1928→WWII.
- Called a *טעאַטער-טרופּע* / *אָפּערעטן-טרופּע* with its own identity.

**Your example:** *טרופּע פֿון קאַדיש-כאַש* — Ensemble (+ Operetta).

### German-Jewish Company
**Plain meaning:** a company pitched at **German-speaking / assimilated** Jewish
audiences — Germanised Yiddish (*daytshmerish*), or working in Germany/Austria.
*This one is hard to read from wording alone — it usually needs your knowledge of the
company. I will flag candidates but not assert it on my own.*

**Signs (weak):** touring in Vienna/Germany, *daytshmerish* spelling in the sources.

**Your example:** *מישוראַטס טרופּע* — German-Jewish (+ Impresario).

### The listed tags you haven't used yet
These exist in the menu but none of your 28 used them. Short plain readings, so we're
agreed on what they'd mean:
- **Cooperative Company** — actors run it jointly, sharing income, no single boss
  (signs: *אַרטעל*, *קאָאָפּעראַטיוו*, shared management).
- **Institutional Company** — the troupe *of* a school, union, party, or *Kultur-lige*
  (signs: a parent body named; often also has a "parent" link in the database).
- **Children's Company** — child performers (*קינדער-טרופּע*).
- **Amateur / Zionist / Socialist / Post-Holocaust / Bilingual** — self-explanatory;
  worth confirming you still want all of these.

## Part B — new tags I'd propose (pick the ones you want)

These come straight out of words the lexicon keeps using that the current menu has no
home for. I'd suggest **8**; the last two are optional.

1. **Wandering Company** *(itinerant)*
   A troupe with **no home stage** — touring is the whole way it exists.
   Signs: *וואַנדער-טרופּע*, *אַרומגעפאָרן איבער שטעט און שטעטלעך*.
   Why: separates the truly homeless touring troupe from a city company that merely
   travels sometimes. *(Would absorb the old "Company on Tour" cases.)*

2. **Provincial Company**
   A company that works **the provinces** — smaller towns around a big-city base.
   Signs: *פּראָווינץ-טרופּע*, *איבער דער פּראָווינץ*, *פּראָווינץ-שטעט*.
   Why: this exact phrase recurs (Sarah Adler's, Korik's) and marks a real, distinct
   circuit of work.

3. **Drama Company** *(spoken/literary theatre)*
   Plays **spoken drama**, as opposed to operetta.
   Signs: *דראַמאַטישע טרופּע*.
   Why: it's the natural partner to "Operetta / Opera," and the phrase appears in the
   sources (Adler's *דראַמאַטישער טרופּע*, Sokolov's).

4. **Art-Theatre Company** *(kunst-teater / literary ensemble)*
   The **art-theatre** current — serious literary repertoire, the *Vilner Trupe* line.
   Signs: *קונסט-טעאַטער*, *אַרטיסטישע גרופּע*, *דראַמאַטישער אַנסאַמבל*.
   Why: the reformed "art" theatre is a genuine category the current menu can't express,
   and it's the opposite pole from the *shund*/operetta troupes.

5. **Kleinkunst / Revue / Cabaret Company**
   **Miniatures, revue, cabaret** — short numbers rather than full plays.
   Signs: *קליינקונסט*, *רעוויו*, *קאַבאַרעט*, a *קוואַרטעט* (*שוואַרץ-קוואַרטעט*).
   Why: retires the awkward old "Kleinkunst" org-type by turning it into a proper tag.

6. **Marionette / Puppet Company**
   A **puppet / marionette** theatre.
   Signs: *מאַריאָנעטן*, *ליאַלקעס*.
   Why: small but unmistakable, and clearly not the same animal as an actors' troupe.

7. **Hebrew Company**
   Performs in **Hebrew** (the *Habima* type), not Yiddish.
   Signs: named as a Hebrew troupe; repertoire in Hebrew.
   Why: a language fact that changes what the company *is*, currently invisible.

8. **Women-Led Company**
   Built around or run by a **woman** (or all-women).
   Signs: a woman's name as the eponym and leader (*סעראַ אַדלער*, *אסתר-רחל קאַמינסקאַ*, *סידי טאָל*).
   Why: worth being able to find these directly; it cuts across Star and Family.

*Optional, if you want finer grain:*

9. **Guest-Star Company** — built around a **visiting star** who plays a short run with
   a local pick-up cast. Signs: *גאַסטראָלן*, *גאַסטראָלירנדיק*. *(Close cousin of Star;
   only worth it if the touring-guest arrangement matters to you.)*

10. **Youth / Student Company** — teenagers or students, often tied to a school or
    movement (distinct from small children). Signs: *יוגנט*, *סטודענטן*, *דראַמאַטישער קרייז*.
