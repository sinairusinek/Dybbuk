Theatre education and Amateur: what was applied, and cases for your review

15 November 2026. Follows the Doc Theatre education and Amateur — categories and audit (2026-11-08), where Sinai answered the open questions today. Please answer in this Doc: write under each case (or comment on it). Nothing in Part C has been written to the database yet.

Part A. What was decided and applied

Sinai accepted the two-question criteria and Rules 1–6 (drama schools move to Theatre education; a studio is Theatre education unless the text shows no pupils; a dramatic society that stages its own plays is Amateur; a circle at a host is its own child record; ליבהאָבער is not always amateur; bare labels are GENERIC). He accepted Mendl Elkin's children's studio as Theatre education, left the ballet school open, and asked for the child records to be made and documented. The definitions in CLASSIFICATION_POLICY.md were updated.

Every proposal was re-read against its sentences before it was applied. Human type decisions were checked against the rules, not accepted automatically. Three bear on this area: Maaty's Amateur for the Kremenchuk workers' studio (3735) agrees with Rule 2: the entry calls it אַרבעטער-ליבהאָבער-טעאַטער and names no pupils. Two contradict a rule, and are in Part C (C10, C20).

Part B. Surveys Sinai asked for

B1. Studios: how many were easy, and where the border is

74 records have “studio” in their name or in their sentences. 9 are not drama studios at all: film studios (Libin 3478, Warner Bros. 3689), a studio's journal (4281), art and painting studios (Murashko 1176, Exter 3822, Kultur-Lige art studio 1235, “18 painters” 3847) and a dance studio (1319). 2 are performing-only workers' studios, Amateur by the Rule 2 exception (Kremenchuk 3735, Tiflis 4360). About 51 were easy: the sentences speak only of pupils, teachers, entering, studying, finishing (Weichert's studio, the Belarusian state studio, the GOSET school, the Kultur-Lige studios, the Artef studio, the Kiev studios). Several of these also staged plays (Granovsky's studio 1919, Rafalsky's studio, Royte Blitn in Bila Tserkva 1928, the Sholem-Aleichem studio, Onheyb in Kiev), but under Rule 2 a student production does not change the type, so they too were easy. Where the theatre the studio became already has its own record (the Sholem-Aleichem studio 1507 and the Sholem-Aleichem Theatre-Studio 4495), the Gimpel two-institution pattern is already in place.

12 were border cases. They cluster around three lines:

  1. Training versus performing as a company. A studio that went on tour or became a theatre (David Herman's studio, the Kiev studio-theatre, the Vilna Theatre Society's studio and troupe). Rule 2 says: two institutions. The open question is where the line falls in the mentions.
  2. “Studio” as the name of a performing group with no visible pupils (Philadelphia youth studio, Idramst, the Spartak workers' theatre studio, the Bamberg DP-camp studio). Rule 2's exception applies only if the text shows no pupils, and these texts are silent either way.
  3. Directing, pedagogy and the university. Meyerhold's State Higher Directing Workshop (1320, applied as Theatre education) was both a school for directors and Meyerhold's laboratory theatre; Piscator's Dramatic Workshop at the New School (2138) was a school that produced plays; the Studiobühne of Münster University (3826) is a university stage. A working line is proposed in C6.

Two of the twelve were settled here: Lodz Hazomir's studio mentions joined the existing studio record (797), and Spartak waits for the club question. The ballet school stays open, as Sinai asked. The rest are in Part C.

B2. Kinds of amateur group: circles, liebhaber, dramatic societies, studios, clubs

After today's changes Amateur holds about 143 records. Sorted by the noun in the name, with what their sentences say:

FormRecordsWhat the contexts show
Circle / section / group (קרייז, סעקציע, גרופּע, קאָלעקטיוו, ווינקל)≈58The commonest form, mostly Eastern Europe 1900s–1940s. More than a quarter of the sentences name a host (ביים / ביי דער): Hazomir, the Bund, Poale Zion, Hashomer Hatzair, the Kultur-Lige, a library, a workers' home, a cooperative colony, an academic culture society. A director is often named (א"פ פֿון), and this is very often where a career begins (debut, first roles). Soviet circles bear dedicatory names (named after Kalinin). A section is always part of a host body.
Liebhaber (ליבהאָבער)≈17The older word: most mentions date 1880s–1920s, in the Russian Empire, Poland and early Argentina. It usually names people rather than an institution, written in quotation marks: מיט אַ גרופּע „ליבהאָבער“, די „ליבהאָבער“ פֿון פּרץ-פֿאַראיין. Several groups wandered the provinces (Migdalski; Piekarnik 1907), i.e. close to semi-professional touring. Russian and Hebrew liebhaber groups occur too (Odessa 1887–89; Habima in Lwów).
Dramatic society / farein (דראַמאַטישע געזעלשאַפֿט, פֿאַראיין; English “society”)≈25A formal association: founders and a chairman (פֿאָרזיצער), a fixed city in the name (Chicago, Kovno, Libau, Brisk, Milwaukee), a regular director for years (Kadison in Kovno, ten years), mergers (Chicago 1917), ambitious repertoire (the Dybbuk, Gordin). Literary-dramatic societies also held readings (Bergelson in Chicago 1929). This is where semi-professional cases sit: Lira in Częstochowa performed with professional actors.
Studio2Only two workers' studios with no pupils (Kremenchuk 1917–20, Tiflis). Every other studio is Theatre education (B1).
Club (דראַמאַטיק קלאָב)≈29Almost wholly American (New York, Chicago, Philadelphia, Boston, Los Angeles, Milwaukee), and named in English: Young Men's, Young Hebrews, Delancey Street. Many are named after a star or a playwright (David Kessler, Jacob Gordin, Morris Morrison, Nathan Goldberg, Hamlet) and so are partly fan clubs. The language is that of membership (מיטגליד, פּרעזידענט). Entin's Progressive Dramatic Club also gave theatre lectures and had pupils (תלמיד), so it reaches into training.
Other named groups≈12Proper-named stages and troupes (Perhift, Loża Lumina, Kometa, Di Bine, Yung-Argentine).

Is the difference only in the name? Mostly yes. Circle, farein, society and club are largely the same thing named in different places and periods: “club” and “society” are the American English-loan forms of the East European “circle” and “farein”, and “liebhaber” is the pre-1914 word that the party and union era replaced with “circle”. Three real differences do run through the material, and none of them is the noun:

  1. Attached or independent. A section or circle at a host versus a free-standing society or club. This is already recorded by parent_db_id (Rule 3), so it needs no subtype.
  2. Status. Purely amateur versus semi-professional: touring liebhaber groups, the “half-professional” troupes (Loża Lumina 2287, Dovke 2308, while the half-professional PIAT 1818 and Philadelphia Folksbine 2849 are typed Theatre), DP-camp sections. This is the form/status split the first Doc mentioned. If a subcategory is wanted, this is the one that carries information.
  3. Club as group versus club as place. The American “dramatic club” is the performing group itself. The East European and Soviet workers' or culture club (the Minsk club Royter Boyer, the Radom Groser-club, Spartak in Buenos Aires) is a host and a venue that has a drama circle. Sinai's “clubs (not as venues)” draws exactly this line.

Proposal: no subtypes by name. If anything, an optional status tag (amateur / semi-professional) on Amateur records, kept like the troupe tags rather than as a type.

B3. Caveats found, and what was done about each

  • ליבהאָבער-ראָלן = lover roles (the amoureux), 13 sentences in the corpus. Only one was mislinked to an Amateur record: the Lemberg Chaverim troupe (2278), now Company on Tour. The others sit correctly on professional troupes (Kompaneets, Sobsey, Axelrad…).
  • קונסט-ליבהאָבער / מוזיק-ליבהאָבער = art or music lovers, i.e. patrons. One such mention, the Minsk „געזעלשאַפֿט פֿון קונסט-ליבהאָבער“ (1901), sits on the Amateur record 446 (the Minsk Yiddish drama society): see C17.
  • “Dramatic” in a name does not mean amateur. Horowitz's Israelitische Dramatische Gesellschaft (Bucharest 1877, db 44), the London Hebrew Dramatic Club of the 1880s (404), and the Fareynikte Dramatishe Kompanye (474) were professional.
  • “Studio” has non-theatre senses: film, art, dance, a journal (B1).
  • “Club” has two senses, group and host/venue (B2.3).
  • Amateur does not record language. Russian amateur circles (3510, 2791, 2818), Russian liebhaber groups (1563, 2294) and Hebrew ones (2289, 2306, 4675) are in Amateur; moving 3510 out of Non-Yiddish Theatre loses that information.
  • Semi-professional groups are spread across Amateur, Theatre and Traveling Company (B2.2).
  • Probable duplicates turned up again (Appendix). None were merged.
