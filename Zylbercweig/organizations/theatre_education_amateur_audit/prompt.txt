The question is the boundary between two types, which reviewers find overlapping:
- "Theatre education": an institution whose PURPOSE is training people for the stage — drama schools, theatre/drama studios with pupils, acting courses, theatre institutes/technikums, drama classes at a conservatory. It may stage student productions; that does not change its type.
- "Amateur": a PERFORMING group of non-professionals — drama circles (דראַמקרייז), dramatic sections (דראַמ.-סעקציע) of a club/party/union/society, ליבהאָבער groups and troupes, "dramatic clubs" (דראַמאַטיק קלאָב), amateur theatre groups. Members may learn by doing; that does not make it a school.
Neighbouring types: "Education" (general schools, universities, rabbinical schools, art/painting/sculpture schools, music conservatories without a drama programme, Yiddish folk schools — NOT drama training); "Theatre" (established professional theatre with a venue); "Traveling Company"/"Company on Tour" (professional troupes); "Theatre-related Society/ Union" (actors' unions, societies that support, coordinate or patronise theatre but do not themselves perform as a group); "Musical organization"; "Jewish political bodies"; and the others in the canonical list.

PRINCIPLE: the type says what the organization IS, never the person's relation to it. A person who "learned to act" in a drama circle does not make the circle a school; a person who "performed" in a studio's production does not make the studio an amateur group.
