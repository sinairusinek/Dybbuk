For Ruthie. Prepared by Sinai, November 2026.

Each question gives what our catalogue records, what Zylbercweig’s Lexicon says (full quotation), and any other source we have. Please write your answer in the box under each question; comments in the margin are welcome too. Where we suggest an answer, “agree” is enough.

  • Lexicon: Zalmen Zylbercweig, Leksikon fun yidishn teater. Each quotation gives volume and printed columns, with a link to the scanned page (volumes 1–6, Yiddish Book Center scans at the Internet Archive: vol. 1, vol. 2, vol. 3, vol. 4, vol. 5, vol. 6). Volume 7 is not online.

Q1. די קאמעליען דאמע: was the ascription to Lateiner, as a translation, wrong?

Question

Is it possible that your ascription of Di kamelyen dame (Dumas fils, La Dame aux camélias), as a translation, to Lateiner was wrong? If so, should the 1896 and 1910 productions below go to another translator (the Lexicon names Max Bukansky and A.-D. Finkel), or to “translator unknown”?

What the catalogue records

  • Catalogue, sheet “Lateiner Plays”, row 54: Expression ID 3839, “Di kamelyen dame”, די קאמעליען דאמע; TAGS: drama; expression: translation; author: 683 (Lateiner); certainty: certain; JPRESS column: “Jpress and Ephemera”. No comment, no source named.

The three sources the catalogue records for the play:

  • Catalogue, sheet “documents/items”, row 140: a poster (כרזה), די קאמעליען דאמע, Windsor Theatre, 1890–1899. NYPL, Thomashefsky Collection, **P (Thomashefsky Collection, Poster No. 66): NYPL item
  • Catalogue, sheet “Lateiner hafakot”, row 88: Production, 26 Oct. 1896, Windsor Theatre, Lower East Side; source: JPRESS, 14 Oct. 1896, p. 3; expression: originalPlay.
  • Catalogue, sheet “Lateiner hafakot”, row 136: Production, 27 Dec. 1910, Kessler’s Lyric Theatre; source: JPRESS, Forverts, 27 Dec. 1910; expression: translation.

Sinai checked these three sources and could not find Lateiner named in any of them. The only other place the catalogue gives an author is Catalogue, sheet “dmitry's”, row 32 (“די קאמעליען דאמע.”, מחבר: לטיינר, no source given); Catalogue, sheet “dmitry's”, row 46 lists the title again with no author.

Q3. די אלמנה, די שטילע חתונה, דער ייד אין סאָביעסקיס צייטן: Hurwitz’s, or plays billed as by “Prof. Yakobi”?

Question

The catalogue lists these three plays among Hurwitz’s. The Lexicon says “Prof. Yakobi” was a name theatres put on plays by various, often anonymous, authors, and it names other authors for all three. Hurwitz was known as “Professor”; could that be how they entered his list? Our suggestion: none of the three is Hurwitz’s. Di almone (1907) is Anshel Shor’s adaptation of Moshe Richter’s Moyshe khayet; Der yid in Sobyeskis tsaytn is Sigmund Feinman’s (later Shabes koydesh); Di shtile khasene (1906) is an anonymous “Prof. Yakobi” play.

What the catalogue records

  • Catalogue, sheet “Hurwitz Plays”, row 112: Expression ID 4049, “Di almone”, operetta, author Hurwitz; comments: “this add https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/frw/1908/01/19/01/article/15.1 attributes the play to Prof. Yakubi.”; year “1907?”; composer “Perlmutter and Wohl”; “Not totally sure this is Hurwitz”; programs: NYPL program 1, NYPL program 2.
  • Catalogue, sheet “Hurwitz hafakot”, row 211: Di almone, production, 24 Jan. 1908, Kalich Theatre; source: the same ad.
  • Catalogue, sheet “Hurwitz music”, row 213, row 214, row 215, row 216, row 217, row 218: Di almone sheet music (Albert Teres Co., 1908; 1914) and a recording, all “Perlmutter and Wohl; words by A. Schorr”. Recording: Di mume glikele, sung by Benny Abelmann.
  • Catalogue, sheet “Hurwitz Plays”, row 109: Expression ID 4046, “Di shtile khasene”, lebensbild, author Hurwitz; no source or comment.
  • Catalogue, sheet “Hurwitz music”, row 195: Di shtile khasene, recording, 1908, “Perlmutter and Wohl; words by Solomon Smulewitz”.
  • Catalogue, sheet “Hurwitz Plays”, row 110: Expression ID 4047, “Der yud in Sabiesky tsayten”, author Hurwitz; expression: “adaptation: Acc. Jaffe, ‘based on Baer Isser Sabiesensky’s novel, The Hard Times’”.
  • Catalogue, sheet “Hurwitz music”, row 200, row 201, row 203: handwritten scores, “Shabes Koydesh … Original title: Der Yid in Sobyeskis Tsaytn, oder, Shabes Koydesh. Historical operetta; 4 acts; 14 nos. Text by Sigmund Feinman” (YIVO Vilna Collection, RG 7).

Ruthie’s answer:

Part 2. Translations and versions

We agreed on a general rule (Sinai, 8 Nov. 2026): when Lateiner’s or Hurwitz’s play is their version of a European play, a production counts as theirs only when the source names them, or when it is their own troupe or theatre at a time their version was in the repertoire. Otherwise it is recorded as a production of the original, translator unknown.
